# Upgrade AI Blog Writer using the MBAUniverse editorial pattern

## Article audit

The reviewed article is approximately **6,351 words** and uses **21 main content headings**, **16 tables**, **153 table rows**, **18 contents links**, and **111 internal links**. Its strongest qualities are:

- A current, specific headline and visible “Last Updated” date.
- A broad topic divided into a logical decision journey: process, updates, eligibility, institutions, exams, locations, specializations, selection advice, and FAQs.
- Tables used for comparable facts such as deadlines, fees, accepted exams, salaries, status, and application links.
- Time-sensitive alerts placed near the relevant decision point.
- Contextual links from entities and user questions to deeper pages.
- A clickable contents list that makes a very long article manageable.
- Facts repeated in different useful views, such as college tier, ROI, location, exam, and specialization.

## Problems we should not copy

- The opening paragraph is too long and repeats several facts.
- Several headings cover overlapping college lists, creating avoidable repetition.
- The same promotional alert block appears four times.
- Many links are repeated, including commercial application links; link quantity is prioritized over reader value.
- Heading hierarchy is inconsistent: most article sections are H3, followed by an H2 FAQ heading.
- Some dates and statements conflict or appear stale, including a 2027 table row containing a 2026 deadline.
- Some tables are too wide for comfortable phone reading.
- Several grammatical errors and dense paragraphs reduce trust and clarity.
- The contents list has 18 items but no grouping, which makes scanning harder.
- The article includes a “Summary” heading, which conflicts with MBA Compass’s existing prohibited-label rule.

## Proposed changes to Write Blog via AI

### 1. Topic architecture before writing
- Convert the approved outline into a reader journey based on search intent, not a generic fixed template.
- Group large topics into 5–8 clear chapters with nested subsections.
- Require each section to answer a distinct question and reject overlapping headings.
- Show the proposed tables, alerts, and internal-link opportunities inside the outline approval step.

### 2. Clearer article opening
- Open with a concise 40–60 word direct answer.
- Follow with a compact facts panel for dates, eligibility, fees, status, or other topic-specific facts.
- Keep the visible update date and state which facts are confirmed, expected, or unavailable.
- Avoid repeated introductory statements and research-process commentary.

### 3. Useful tables, not mandatory filler
- Generate tables when verified comparable data exists.
- Select columns from the topic: date, fee, eligibility, exam, status, result, comparison, advantage, limitation, or action.
- Require a short explanation before each table and a practical interpretation after it.
- Split oversized tables into logical groups and make them usable on phones.
- Never invent cells; show “Not announced” or omit the row when evidence is unavailable.

### 4. Alerts and highlighted information
- Add semantic callouts for Deadline, Update, Important, Caution, Eligibility, and Action Required.
- Use alerts only for high-value information, with a default maximum based on article length.
- Attach each alert to the relevant section instead of repeating one promotional block throughout the article.
- Continue bolding verified dates, fees, percentages, percentiles, rankings, salaries, counts, and short decision-critical facts.

### 5. Better contents navigation
- Build the contents list from approved H2/H3 headings after final content cleanup.
- Use stable unique anchors, smooth scrolling, fixed-header offset, and active-section highlighting.
- Group long contents lists into chapters and allow collapse/expand on phones.
- Add “Back to contents” navigation after long sections.
- Validate every contents link before a draft can pass the publishing checklist.

### 6. Internal-linking rules
- Suggest links by entity and reader intent: exams, colleges, locations, comparisons, calculators, application pages, and related guides.
- Use descriptive anchor text rather than repeated “read more” links.
- Limit links per section, avoid duplicate destinations, and prevent links from interrupting every sentence.
- Prefer MBA Compass pages that genuinely answer the next user question.
- Keep supplied third-party references in the editor evidence panel; do not turn them into repetitive public attribution or promotional links.

### 7. Clarity and editorial validation
- Enforce short paragraphs, direct section openings, descriptive headings, focused lists, and one purpose per section.
- Detect repeated claims, duplicate sections, conflicting dates/figures, stale year references, grammar problems, and unsupported claims.
- Preserve the required paragraphs, tables, pointers, FAQs, overview, and Important Points while keeping “Summary”, “Key”, and “Snapshot” out of generated labels.
- Continue saving every generated article as a draft until the editor approves the outline, figures, freshness, links, and final checklist.

### 8. Editor controls and quality report
- Show a structure report with section coverage, table count, alert count, link count, contents-link validation, repeated-content warnings, and mobile-table warnings.
- Let the editor add, remove, reorder, or change the type of planned tables and alerts before generation.
- Show each internal-link suggestion with its intended section and anchor text before insertion.
- Keep the existing source comparison and freshness review, adding explicit stale/conflicting-data blockers.

## Expected result

MBA Compass articles will adopt the reference article’s depth, decision-led sections, comparison tables, alerts, contents navigation, and contextual linking while improving concision, mobile readability, factual consistency, and editorial trust. The system will reproduce the editorial method, not the third party’s wording, layout, branding, or promotional patterns.
